## 1. What Is Convolution?

Convolution tells us how all earlier pieces of an input combine to create the output now.

%%KC_BLOCK%%<section class="convolution-editorial-block convolution-block-input" data-convolution-block="p1-memory" data-convolution-layout="editorial" data-convolution-page="1" data-convolution-number="01"><span class="convolution-page-marker" aria-hidden="true">01</span><div class="convolution-block-body"><h3>A system remembers</h3><ul><li>Each small piece of the <span class="convolution-key convolution-key-input">past input</span> creates its own delayed response.</li><li>The <span class="convolution-key convolution-key-output">output now</span> is the sum of every response still present.</li></ul></div></section>%%KC_END%%

%%KC_BLOCK%%<section class="convolution-editorial-block convolution-analogy-block convolution-analogy-block-ink" data-convolution-block="p1-ink" data-convolution-layout="editorial" data-convolution-page="1" data-convolution-number="02" data-convolution-analogy="ink"><span class="convolution-page-marker" aria-hidden="true">02</span><div class="convolution-analogy-copy"><p class="convolution-analogy-kicker">Picture it</p><h3>Ink in a transparent pool</h3><ul><li>You add ink one drop at a time, not as one bucket.</li><li>Each drop spreads and fades, but it does not vanish at once.</li><li>The color now comes from every earlier drop still left in the water.</li></ul></div><figure class="convolution-analogy-figure"><img class="lesson-img convolution-analogy-image" src="/lesson-illustrations/2_4-2/convolution-ink-memory-v2.png" data-api-asset="1" alt="Ink drops spreading and accumulating in a transparent pool" width="1153" height="2048" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure></section>%%KC_END%%

%%KC_BLOCK%%<section class="convolution-editorial-block convolution-block-response" data-convolution-block="p1-symbol-map" data-convolution-layout="editorial" data-convolution-page="1" data-convolution-number="03"><span class="convolution-page-marker" aria-hidden="true">03</span><div class="convolution-block-body"><h3>Map the pool back to the formula</h3><div class="convolution-equation"><span class="convolution-key convolution-key-output">\(c(t)\)</span> \(=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\) <span class="convolution-key convolution-key-input">\(x(\tau)\)</span><span class="convolution-key convolution-key-response">\(g(t-\tau)\)</span> \(d\tau\)</div><ul><li><span class="convolution-key convolution-key-input">\(x(\tau)\)</span> is the ink added at past time \(\tau\).</li><li><span class="convolution-key convolution-key-response">\(g(t-\tau)\)</span> is how much influence that drop still has now.</li><li>The integral adds every drop's current contribution.</li></ul></div></section>%%KC_END%%

## 2. Why Use Graphical Convolution?

The picture is useful when the formula alone hides what the two signals are doing.

## 3. How to Flip and Slide

Keep the input still and turn the system response into the signal that moves.
